A Backup Standard for the Archive

How many copies, how far apart, and held by whom — the preservation field's settled answers, mapped to Pergamon's three threats: catastrophe, censorship, and institutional loss.

The Medium

Why it fits on tape

One LTO-9 cartridge holds 18 TB. A complete copy of today's open-weight frontier — every major model from the 1.5B class up to the 1.6-trillion-parameter DeepSeek V4 Pro — comes to roughly 8.6 TB at full precision: under half of a single cartridge. Trained weights are near-incompressible, so native capacity is the honest figure; tape's hardware compression buys nothing on model data. Off the drive a cartridge is air-gapped and immutable by default, rated for about thirty years, at the lowest cost per terabyte of any medium and zero idle power. One full copy is a single, half-used cartridge — and seven copies is seven cartridges. The constraint was never storage.

01 — Redundancy

How many copies?

Guards against media failure and catastrophe. The field's thresholds run from baseline IT practice up to preservation grade:

3-2-1 · baseline

Three copies, on two media types, with one off-site. Endorsed by CISA and NIST — the floor, not the goal.

3-2-1-1-0 · modern

Adds one air-gapped or immutable copy, and zero errors — proven by routine restore tests.

NDSA Level 4 · preservation

At least three complete copies, in locations with different disaster threats.

LOCKSS · enhancement

Four or more — and, by design, many more — so consensus among independent, mutually-distrusting copies defines truth, with no central authority to corrupt.

Tape is WORM and air-gapped by nature, so every copy already satisfies the modern rule's “+1 offline / immutable” leg for free.

Pergamon target

≥ 7 independent tape masters — an odd quorum that can outvote two corrupted copies and still hold a majority with several offline · each WORM + air-gapped · fixity-checked on a fixed cadence (the “0”).

02 — Separation

How far apart?

Distance is only a proxy. The real target: no single event — quake, flood, grid failure, or a government order — can reach two copies at once. Separate by disaster-threat zone, and, against censorship, by legal jurisdiction.

Pergamon target

≥ 3 different disaster-threat zones, spanning ≥ 2 legal jurisdictions — at least one beyond the reach of the likeliest censoring state.

03 — Stewardship

Which institutions?

If one organization holds every copy, survival depends on that organization's funding and politics — historically a larger cause of real-world loss than any hardware failure (LOCKSS). Mix institution types so financial, legal, and political failure modes don't correlate.

National Library

Holds: a durable legal-deposit mandate. Exposed to: alignment with the same state that may censor.

University Library

Holds: mission alignment, global spread. Exposed to: budget and funding-cycle volatility.

Independent Nonprofit

Holds: a pure mission and agility. Exposed to: financial fragility and litigation.

Government Archive

Holds: a funded public mandate. Exposed to: political capture and policy reversal.

Community / Volunteer

Holds: a censorship-resistant swarm. Exposed to: coordination and continuity risk.

Commercial Cold Storage

Holds: deep SLAs and scale. Exposed to: lock-in, price hikes, discontinuation.

Crossing borders makes recruitment a small act of diplomacy — and that is the point. Each steward is an independent partner, not a branch office; the same breadth that makes the archive hard to seize makes it hard to capture.

Pergamon target

≥ 3 distinct institution types · ≥ 1 mission-driven nonprofit · ≥ 1 steward outside the censoring jurisdiction · never single-org or single-state custody.

The Standard, Grounded

Measured against the LOCKSS model

Stanford's LOCKSS program — Lots Of Copies Keep Stuff Safe — is the closest working model to what Pergamon is building, and the clearest account of what an archival standard actually requires. Its founding premise is bracing: the greatest threat to digital information is not media decay or format obsolescence but ourselves — deliberate and accidental human action, and the failure of the organizations meant to be custodians. That is exactly why Pergamon's named threats are censorship and institutional loss, not catastrophe alone. LOCKSS frames a robust system as six questions; here is how the standard answers each.

01Is it built on a realistic threat model?

Yes. The standard plans for catastrophe (media failure and natural disaster), censorship (deliberate, targeted human action), and institutional loss (the slow erosion of funding and political will) — the same human and organizational threats LOCKSS foregrounds, rather than treating hardware as the only enemy.

02Does it avoid a single, corruptible fixity store?

Yes — the load-bearing principle. There is no canonical hash registry whose compromise would quietly poison every copy. Fixity is the consensus of the seven independent, mutually-distrusting masters: the content vouches for itself, by agreement, at each audit. A central authority of truth is precisely the single point of failure the standard refuses.

03Is it decentralized, not merely distributed?

Yes. Seven copies are worthless if one organization, one jurisdiction, or one administrator can reach them all. So the standard demands at least three kinds of steward — national and university libraries, an independent nonprofit, a government archive, a community swarm, with commercial cold storage as one leg only — spread across at least two jurisdictions. No single party holds the whole.

04Does it routinely validate and repair?

Yes — the “0” of 3-2-1-1-0. The masters are not a dark archive no one opens until an emergency. On a fixed cadence each copy's fixity is read and compared against the others, and outliers are repaired from the majority. Data that is never checked is data you cannot trust.

05Does it fail gracefully — slowly, not fast?

Yes. Following LOCKSS, repair is deliberately conservative: disagreement is surfaced, but a copy is corrected only on overwhelming consensus, leaving time to investigate. This is why seven matters — an odd quorum can outvote two simultaneously corrupted copies, so tampering is caught and contained before bad bits can cascade across the whole archive.

06Does it preserve local custody and control?

Yes. Each steward holds and governs its own master on open, non-proprietary media — LTO tape and open formats — beholden to no service provider's business model or terms of use. It is the reason commercial cold storage can be one leg of the seven, but never the spine.

The Pergamon Standard

≥ 7 fixity-verified tape masters · ≥ 3 disaster-threat zones · ≥ 2 jurisdictions · ≥ 3 institution types.

  • Catastrophe → copies + zones
  • Censorship → jurisdictions
  • Institutional loss → institutional diversity

Seven masters — one for each of the classical planets — an odd quorum scattered across the Earth and its jurisdictions, that no single power can quietly close.
