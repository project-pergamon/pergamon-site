A Public Archive for AI

Preserving open-source AI so it can be rebuilt after censorship, catastrophe, or institutional loss.

Join the founding circleSupport the archive

Project Pergamon is a public-interest effort to preserve open-source Large Language Models (LLMs) as part of the historical record.

We are building a professional archive for open-source AI: a practical preservation effort with the standards of a scholarly institution and the urgency of a historical record at risk of being lost.

Why this exists

Open-source AI models increasingly rival closed ones in capability, while offering greater freedom, economy, and privacy to users. But open models are fragile. They can be delisted, neglected, censored, fragmented across platforms, or rendered unusable when the surrounding technical context disappears.

What we preserve

Project Pergamon exists to preserve the possibility of reconstructing frontier, open-source LLMs. We seek to document the full set of materials needed to understand and rebuild historically significant models from durable archival records, including

  • model weights
  • architecture and configuration
  • tokenizer and related metadata
  • provenance and integrity information
  • documentation for future custodians

Pergamon's approach to archiving these documents is grounded in established digital preservation standards: multiple copies, air-gapped media, fixity checks, and institional diversity. Our long-term goal is to establish a durable preservation infrastructure that is hard for any single institution or jurisdiction to suppress, and that improves the odds of future technical and historical recovery in the event of catastrophy.

How we do it

Project Pergamon begins by transferring models' full reconstruction stack onto the Linear-Tape Open [LTO] format. Today, LTO represents the enterprise-grade archival tape standard used in real preservation workflows.

LTO is our first archival substrate because it is the proven, scaleable, energy-efficent solution for professional archives. But LTO-9 is not a forever medium. Real preservation depends on redundancy, verification, migration, and institutional continuity over time - considerations that are central to what we do.

We are commitmted to exploring more durable ceramic- and silica-based as they become commercially viable for storing model weights.

The Archimedes Series

Our first public archive proofs will appear under The Archimedes Series.

The name draws lightly on the Archimedes Palimpsest: a reminder that technical knowledge can survive damage, obscurity, and time if enough of the record remains to be recovered.

We are prioritizing public legibility for this first proof: models that clearly show why preserving open-source AI matters to professionals, historians, and policy-makers.

The first proof in the Archimedes Series will be a small but credible preservation milestone built around a curated list of historically-significant 1.5B-class open-weight models, with candidates including:

  • DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B
  • Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct
  • SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct

Final selection will be informed by collaborators across open-model engineering, archival systems, and digital preservation.

We are also seeking a technical collaborator to help co-author a whitepaper on the design of the first preservation pass and its path to scale.

Who we are

Project Pergamon was founded by Zachary Sheldon, a linguistic and cultural anthropologist. His current research examines the deep history of algorithmic computing, with a focus on early modern Arabic mathematics and esotericism. His background spans preservation research, startup AI work, and institution-building through organizing and nonprofit collaboration.

Zachary Sheldon built Project Pergamon with help from Aquarius (peopleaquarius.com), where he leads research on how people use AI-native tools to move complex projects from idea to execution.

How to help

Project Pergamon is seeking founding support to launch the first Archimedes proof and the public materials around it. Early support will help fund purchase of LTO-9 materials and secure storage and the design of an initial 50-year preservation workflow. This project will generate public media documentatio for journalists and educators, opening doors to institutional partners at universities, libraries, and national archives.

If you want to help build a durable public record for open-source AI, you can support the project as a donor, advisor, technical collaborator, or institutional partner.
